# Six Months of Fridays Off: What Our Four-Day Week Taught Us

From January to June 2026, our 12-person design and development team worked four days a week. Fridays off, same pay, and no extra hours Monday to Thursday. In July we made it permanent.

If the idea makes you nervous about output, the numbers might help. We shipped 14 client projects during the trial, against 13 in the same period last year. Sick days fell from 31 to 18. And our last job ad drew three times as many applicants as before.

We're not the only ones to see results like this. In a UK pilot that ran from June to December 2022, at least 56 of the 61 participating organisations kept the four-day week afterwards, and sick days fell by 65% ([Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/four-day-week-trial-results-uk/)). Revenue at those employers rose by 1.4% on average over the trial, and 71% of employees reported lower levels of burnout ([Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/four-day-week-trial-results-uk/)). Firms that volunteer for a trial are probably the ones keenest to make it work, so treat any headline figure, ours included, with some caution [check].

## Don't squeeze five days into four

Our first attempt was ten-hour days from Monday to Thursday. We dropped it after three weeks because people were exhausted. The point is fewer hours, not the same hours packed into a smaller box. If your plan depends on everyone working longer days, you're running a compressed week, and it may wear people out rather than rest them [check].

## Cut meetings first

Priya, our ops lead, put it bluntly: "the meetings were the real problem, not the hours." We moved our standup to async updates in Slack and cut the weekly all-hands from 60 minutes to 30. A shared calendar now shows who's off on any given day.

If you're considering a shorter week, start by auditing your regular meetings. You can do that before you change anyone's hours, and it tells you quickly where the time is going.

## Plan for your clients

Not everyone found it easy. Two people in client-facing roles struggled, because clients still expected replies on Fridays. Our fix was a rota for account managers: one covers Friday and takes Monday off instead. Without a plan like this, the people closest to your customers are the ones most likely to keep working on their day off [check].

Think about when your customers expect to reach you. Say you run a small agency whose clients often email on a Friday afternoon. A rota means someone is always there to answer, while each person still works four days.

## What we didn't measure

We didn't track the cost of the change, including any effect on overtime. Overtime is the one to watch: if work simply spills into paid extra hours, gains elsewhere could disappear [check]. If you run your own trial, measure cost from day one, alongside output, sick days and hiring, so the final decision rests on evidence rather than impressions.

For us, six months was enough. Output held, fewer people were off sick, and hiring got easier. The four-day week is staying.
